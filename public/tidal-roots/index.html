Tidal Roots

Every root replanted here holds back a little more carbon, a little more coastline, and a little more of the life that depends on both. Scroll to walk the delta.

Scroll
Act One

A coastline losing ground

Decades of clearing left this stretch of delta bare — the roots that once held the silt together were gone, and the tide kept taking a little more of the bank every year.

Act Two

Roots go back in the mud

Local nurseries raise the seedlings; planting crews walk them out at low tide, row by row, into ground chosen for exactly the water depth each species needs to take hold.

Act Three

The canopy returns

Within a few seasons the first stands close ranks overhead. Fish nurseries reappear in the shade of the roots; the birds that had moved on start coming back.

Act Four

A delta that feeds itself again

Households along the bank now draw a living from the forest they replanted — honey, fish, and a wage for tending trees that are quietly banking carbon for the rest of us.

How a tonne gets here

01

Nursery

Seedlings are raised from local seed stock until their roots can hold their own weight.

02

Planting

Crews plant at low tide, spacing each seedling for the species and the site's specific hydrology.

03

Monitoring

Growth, survival, and carbon stock are measured on a fixed schedule against the site baseline.

04

Verification

An independent body checks the numbers before anything is issued.

05

Credit issued

One verified tonne becomes one credit, traceable back to this stretch of delta.
